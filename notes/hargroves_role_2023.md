# The Role of Distributed Energy Resources and Associated Business Models in the Decentralised Energy Transition: A Review | Project Pillar: Literature Review
**Date:** May 2026
**Status:** Peer-Reviewed Literature

## 🎯 Executive Summary
This review paper explores the technical configurations and emerging business models associated with Distributed Energy Resources (DERs) in the transition to decentralized grids. Leveraging findings from Australia's RACE for 2030 Cooperative Research Centre, the authors argue that the transition's primary bottlenecks are strategic and regulatory rather than technical.

## 🔑 Key Arguments & Findings
* **Finding 1:** The decentralization of the electricity sector converts passive consumers into "prosumers" who actively produce, store, and manage energy, challenging traditional centralized utility business models and billing structures.
* **Finding 2:** Operational grid disruption is not an inherent trait of DER technologies but rather a consequence of a lack of strategic responses, outdated regulatory frameworks, and mixed signals sent to policymaking bodies.

## 🛠️ Methodological Notes / Technical Specifications
* **Study Type:** Strategic literature review covering academic and industry papers from 2002 to 2023.
* **DER Technologies Cataloged:** Solar PV, chemical batteries, thermal storage, electric vehicles (EVs ranging from 65 kWh to 500 kWh), fuel cells, co/tri-generation, biomass, gas/diesel generators, and micro-hydro.
* **Research Affiliation:** Renewable, Affordable, Clean Energy Cooperative Research Centre (RACE CRC), Australia.

## 💡 Personal Insights & Open Questions
* **Potential Application:** The business models described emphasize the need for EGoT's Energy Service Interfaces (ESIs) to support flexible, service-oriented transactions (such as capacity booking or telemetry reporting) to accommodate third-party aggregators.
* **Gap/Next Step:** While the review identifies strategic leverage points, it does not propose specific communications software standards (like IEEE 2030.5 or OpenADR) or API structures to operationalize these transactional models.

## 📌 Critical Quotes
> "The shift from large-scale centralised energy systems to smaller scale decentralised systems based on Distributed Energy Resources (DER) is likely to cause a sector-wide replacement of current electricity management practices and business models—creating a new energy paradigm."
> "If disruption is experienced it will be due to a lack of strategic responses rather than the nature of the technology."
