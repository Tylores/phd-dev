# Assessing customer engagement in electricity distribution-level flexibility product provision: The Norwegian case | Project Pillar: Case Study
**Date:** May 2026
**Status:** Peer-Reviewed Literature

## 🎯 Executive Summary
This paper analyzes how explicit flexibility instruments (specifically, demand response programs) interact with alternative distribution network tariffs (static vs. dynamic) to shape long-term customer engagement. Using 2019 pilot project data from Skagerak Energi in Norway, the authors model customer decisions and demonstrate that dynamic network tariffs significantly enhance the efficacy of demand response programs, leading to deferred network reinforcements and improved system-wide economic efficiency.

## 🔑 Key Arguments & Findings
* **Finding 1:** The efficacy of demand response programs (explicit flexibility procurement) is highly dependent on and enhanced by the simultaneous implementation of dynamic network tariffs (implicit economic signals), specifically those featuring a Peak Coincidence Network Charge.
* **Finding 2:** Relying on a single flexibility instrument or ignoring the interplay between tariffs and contracts can result in suboptimal customer engagement and inefficient grid planning, whereas combined policy designs successfully defer network reinforcements and lower total system costs.

## 🛠️ Methodological Notes / Technical Specifications
* **Data Source:** Actual 2019 hourly load data and parameters from the Skagerak Energi pilot site in Norway (part of the E-Regio project).
* **Optimization Model:** Linear programming/optimization model simulating cost-minimizing customer behavior, including options for voluntary load curtailment and investment in Distributed Energy Resources (specifically battery storage).
* **Tariff Structures Tested:** 
  1. Standard static tariff (fixed and volumetric charges).
  2. Dynamic distribution tariff (fixed and critical peak/Peak Coincidence Network Charges).

## 💡 Personal Insights & Open Questions
* **Potential Application:** The insights on dynamic network tariffs and demand response interactions can directly inform the implementation and coordination of grid services within the EGoT platform, especially the End Device (EDevice) and DER management systems.
* **Gap/Next Step:** The study assumes fully rational customers aiming purely to minimize network-related bills. Investigating how behavioral limitations, varying levels of automation, and non-economic factors (e.g., convenience, user fatigue) affect these results in practice is a crucial next step.

## 📌 Critical Quotes

> "When an explicit flexibility instrument is added (e.g., a Demand Response program or a Flexibility Market), the interplay between the two instruments will affect customer engagement, and ultimately the efficacy and cost-effectiveness of flexibility procurement."
