# Comprehensive Overview of Power System Flexibility during the Scenario of High Penetration of Renewable Energy in Utility Grid | Project Pillar: Literature Review
**Date:** May 2026
**Status:** Peer-Reviewed Literature

## 🎯 Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive overview of power system flexibility (PSF) under high variable renewable energy (VRE) penetration (exceeding 80%). It classifies and reviews over 120 papers across definitions, drivers, resources, requirement estimation, assessment metrics, measurement methods, and flexibility planning.

## 🔑 Key Arguments & Findings
* **Finding 1:** Power system flexibility (PSF) is defined as the system's ability to reliably and cost-effectively manage the variability and uncertainty of demand and supply across all timescales. As VRE exceeds 80%, long-duration storage and demand-side management become mandatory.
* **Finding 2:** Flexibility resources include flexible generation (gas, hydro), storage systems, grid-side flexibility (FACTS, HVDC), and demand-side management (smart charging of EVs, HVAC demand response). System-level coordination and aggregation of these resources are key to cost-effective transition.

## 🛠️ Methodological Notes / Technical Specifications
* Comprehensive review of 123 references.
* Organizes PSF into 9 categories (concepts, definitions/indicators, drivers, resources, requirements, metrics, measurement methods, planning/operational provisions, and generation expansion planning).
* Reviews various analytical models such as Unit Commitment (UC), Economic Dispatch (ED), robust optimization, and stochastic programming used for flexibility assessment.

## 💡 Personal Insights & Open Questions
* **Potential Application:** Serves as a theoretical framework for EGoT's dissertation background chapter to define "flexibility" and justify the use of IEEE 2030.5 DER coordination as a key demand-side flexibility resource.
* **Gap/Next Step:** While the review classifies modeling tools, it lacks a detailed comparison of the computational complexity and real-time execution feasibility of these different flexibility estimation models on actual distribution feeders.
