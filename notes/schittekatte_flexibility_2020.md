# Flexibility markets: Q&A with project pioneers | Project Pillar: Case Study
**Date:** May 2026
**Status:** Peer-Reviewed Literature

## 🎯 Executive Summary
This paper analyzes four pioneering local flexibility market platforms—Piclo Flex, Enera, GOPACS, and NODES—using a six-question design framework. It highlights how these platforms coordinate distributed energy resources (DERs) to manage grid congestion and defer expensive network reinforcement.

## 🔑 Key Arguments & Findings
* **Finding 1:** Operating local flexibility markets through independent third parties helps avoid conflicts of interest, ensures transparency, and prevents market foreclosure for competing grid services.
* **Finding 2:** Significant structural differences exist between platforms regarding whether they offer reservation payments to guarantee capacity, standardise products, and how they coordinate transmission (TSO) and distribution (DSO) requests to avoid conflicting actions.

## 🛠️ Methodological Notes / Technical Specifications
* **Taxonomy/Framework:** 6-question framework addressing: (1) wholesale integration, (2) third-party operators, (3) reservation payments, (4) product standardization, (5) TSO-DSO cooperation, and (6) DSO-DSO cooperation.
* **Platforms Compared:** Piclo Flex (UK), Enera (Germany), GOPACS (Netherlands), and NODES (Norway/Europe).

## 💡 Personal Insights & Open Questions
* **Potential Application:** Designing market endpoints or reservation protocols in the EGoT platform, such as FlowReservation or DERProgram interfaces, to coordinate local DER dispatch and ensure DSO network constraints are respected.
* **Gap/Next Step:** The study focuses on business models and regulatory sandboxes but does not examine the telemetry requirements or protocol standards (such as IEEE 2030.5 or IEEE 1547) needed to execute and verify these local market dispatches.

## 📌 Critical Quotes
> "Differences among the projects are found in the extent to which the flexibility markets are integrated into other existing markets, the use of reservation payments, the use of standardized products, and the way TSO-DSO cooperation is done."
