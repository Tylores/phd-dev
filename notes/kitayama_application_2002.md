# Application of data mining to customer profile analysis in the power electric industry | Project Pillar: Case Study
**Date:** May 2026
**Status:** Peer-Reviewed Literature

## 🎯 Executive Summary
This paper outlines the application of data mining (specifically decision trees) for customer segmentation and profiling in deregulated retail electricity markets. By dividing customers based on consumption volume and load factor, utilities can target preferred customers with custom tariff structures and optimize marketing/efficiency strategies.

## 🔑 Key Arguments & Findings
* **Finding 1:** Retail utility deregulation changes the relationship between utilities and customers. Utilities must transition from uniform service to Customer Relationship Management (CRM) by categorizing customers into segments like "preferred" (high consumption, high load factor) and "regular."
* **Finding 2:** Traditional retail data mining metrics like RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) do not apply to electricity, which is continuously supplied. Instead, using decision trees with parameters like contract type, load factor, and industry type helps identify and predict preferred customer profiles.

## 🛠️ Methodological Notes / Technical Specifications
* Customer Segmentation Map using two attributes: annual electricity consumption and load factor.
* Analytical CRM process including customer segmentation, service menu planning, income analysis, and service response analysis.
* Classification using entropy-minimizing decision trees.

## 💡 Personal Insights & Open Questions
* **Potential Application:** Shows the historical evolution of utility customer profiling. In EGoT, customer load profiling (using AMI data) is key for tariff and DER program design. Decision tree methods are simple, explainable techniques to group customers into dispatchable DER cohorts.
* **Gap/Next Step:** The paper operates on very coarse monthly/annual metrics (e.g., average load factor). How do modern high-resolution smart meter (AMI) time-series data change this segmentation when dealing with dynamic behind-the-meter PV and storage?
