# Progress and prospects of thermo-mechanical energy storage—a critical review | Project Pillar: Literature Review
**Date:** May 2026
**Status:** Peer-Reviewed Literature

## 🎯 Executive Summary
This critical review evaluates the thermodynamic principles, recent developments, and thermo-economic competitiveness of grid-scale thermo-mechanical energy storage (TMES) technologies, including compressed-air energy storage (CAES), liquid-air energy storage (LAES), and pumped-thermal electricity storage (PTES). It highlights their potential for high round-trip efficiencies (>60%) and long lifetimes (>30 years) at large capacities.

## 🔑 Key Arguments & Findings
* **Finding 1:** While pumped-hydro energy storage (PHES) dominates bulk storage, it is geographically limited, and chemical batteries suffer from high costs and short lifetimes, highlighting the importance of developing and utilizing location-independent TMES options.
* **Finding 2:** At larger discharge power ratings and longer durations, TMES systems offer low specific costs (often below 100 $/kWh), long operational lifetimes (>30 years), and unique sector-coupling opportunities (e.g. heating and cooling) despite lower efficiency compared to batteries.

## 🛠️ Methodological Notes / Technical Specifications
* **Technologies Analyzed:** CAES (Compressed-Air), LAES (Liquid-Air), PTES (Pumped-Thermal).
* **Performance / Cost Metrics:** Round-trip efficiency exceeding 60%, lifetime over 30 years, specific energy costs below 100 $/kWh.
* **Methodology:** Integrated thermo-economic comparison of component costs across different system sizes using chemical process cost indices (CEPCI).

## 💡 Personal Insights & Open Questions
* **Potential Application:** Although EGoT focuses primarily on residential-scale distributed assets (batteries, water heaters, PV), understanding bulk TMES options is crucial when designing transactive energy markets where micro-grid aggregations must interface with bulk system operators.
* **Gap/Next Step:** The review points out that PTES and LAES are still in early stages of commercial deployment (low TRL) compared to PHES and lithium-ion batteries. What are the specific grid service integration hurdles (e.g., response latency) for these large thermo-mechanical systems compared to fast-acting battery aggregations?
