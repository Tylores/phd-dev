# The Power of Focal Points Is Limited: Even Minute Payoff Asymmetry May Yield Large Coordination Failures | Project Pillar: Literature Review
**Date:** May 2026
**Status:** Peer-Reviewed Literature

## 🎯 Executive Summary
This document is the web appendix containing experimental instructions for a study on focal points and coordination. The authors show that while focal points (such as landmark locations) successfully guide players to coordinate in symmetric games, the introduction of even minor payoff asymmetries (e.g., slight discrepancies in rewards for matching) disrupts this salience and causes significant coordination failures.

## 🔑 Key Arguments & Findings
* **Finding 1:** In symmetric matching games, salience-based focal points (e.g., Sears Tower vs. AT&T Building) serve as highly effective coordinating mechanisms for independent agents.
* **Finding 2:** When even a tiny payoff asymmetry is introduced (biasing the payoffs of one matching outcome toward Player 1 and the other toward Player 2), the coordination power of focal points is severely degraded, leading to widespread coordination failures.

## 🛠️ Methodological Notes / Technical Specifications
* **Experimental Design:** Two-person matching coordination games with labeled choices (e.g., the Chicago Skyscrapers Game featuring Sears Tower and the AT&T Building across the street).
* **Payoff Structure:** 
  - Symmetric treatment: Both players receive $100 for matching on either choice.
  - Asymmetric treatment: Player 1 gets $100 and Player 2 gets $110 for Sears Tower; Player 1 gets $110 and Player 2 gets $100 for AT&T Building. Zero payoff for mismatch.
* **Sample / Context:** Run with University of Chicago students; Appendix provides raw questionnaire templates and instructions.

## 💡 Personal Insights & Open Questions
* **Potential Application:** Crucial for multi-agent game-theoretic controls in EGoT. When distributed energy resources (DERs) are asked to coordinate (e.g., voluntary curtailment) without explicit communication, minor differences in their local financial rewards or battery degradation costs can lead to massive coordination failures. This highlights the absolute necessity of EGoT's structured scheduling APIs over purely decentralized, incentive-based guess-work.
* **Gap/Next Step:** The study focuses on zero-communication coordination. An important next step is analyzing the minimal bandwidth or negotiation protocols (like SEP 2.0 FlowReservation) required to restore 100% coordination under asymmetric payoff distributions.
