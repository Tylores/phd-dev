# A Comprehensive Review of Thermal Energy Storage | Project Pillar: Literature Review
**Date:** May 2026
**Status:** Peer-Reviewed Literature

## 🎯 Executive Summary
This paper provides a detailed review of thermal energy storage (TES) technologies, classifying them into sensible, latent (using phase-change materials), and thermo-chemical systems. It evaluates their performance, calculation of storage capacities, and cost-effectiveness for enhancing solar energy utilization and building efficiency.

## 🔑 Key Arguments & Findings
* **Finding 1:** Thermal energy storage is a key enabling technology for bridging the gap between renewable energy supply and demand, improving overall system efficiency and reducing greenhouse gas emissions.
* **Finding 2:** Latent-heat storage (LHS) using phase-change materials (PCMs) offers high storage density and near-isothermal operation, which can optimize space constraints in building installations compared to traditional sensible water tanks.

## 🛠️ Methodological Notes / Technical Specifications
* **Technologies Analyzed:** Sensible Heat Storage (SHS) (water tanks, underground thermal energy storage, packed beds); Latent-Heat Storage (LHS) (PCMs); Thermo-Chemical Storage (TCS) (chemical reactions/adsorption).
* **Design/Simulation Tools:** Mentions Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) using FLUENT software as a widely used tool for optimizing TES systems.
* **Temperature Classifications:** Defines hot storage (80–90°C), warm storage (40–50°C), and cold storage (7–15°C).

## 💡 Personal Insights & Open Questions
* **Potential Application:** Modeling aggregated thermal energy storage systems (such as residential water heaters or building HVAC systems with thermal storage) as controllable loads or virtual batteries for demand response programs.
* **Gap/Next Step:** While the paper details materials and thermodynamic properties, it does not explore standard control communication protocols (such as IEEE 2030.5) required to integrate TES into a modern smart grid infrastructure.

## 📌 Critical Quotes
> "TES not only reduces the discrepancy between the demand and supply by conserving energy, but also improves the performance and thermal reliability of the system."
